Sources and notes

Race calls and winners come from Wikipedia's House, Senate and governor results pages (CC BY-SA 4.0), which follow AP calls and can trail them by a few hours. Shading is by winner only; these tables do not include vote shares.

House hexagons are generated per state from each state's seat count and rough location, so a district's position inside its state is approximate. "Party gain" follows Wikipedia, and includes seats that changed hands because district lines were redrawn.

Turnout: UF Election Lab (Michael McDonald), ballots counted as a share of the voting-eligible population. 2026 figures are early estimates until states certify; 2022 figures are final.

State and county boundaries: US Census Bureau, via us-atlas. Population: 2020 Census state totals, spread across counties using each county's share in earlier Census estimates. County-level vote counts are not shown because they aren't available yet. County lines date from 2017: Connecticut shows its former 8 counties (replaced by 9 planning regions in 2022), and Alaska's Valdez-Cordova area appears as one before its 2019 split.
